[Noah Pepper](https://x.com/noahmp/status/1943141158261248347) asked on X:

> The product at @mercury is good - but the customer service really shines - consistently.
>
> I'm curious what @immad is doing differently in CX from other fintechs.
>
> I've never had a bad interaction with the team across several years of using them for both personal and business.

[My reply](https://x.com/JakeKeuhlen/status/1943423882486255983):

I can share some of the Mercury lore here, but the tldr is that since almost the very beginning, we've treated support as an element of our overall Product surface.

As we grew, the second team that was split off into its own domain was our "Support Product" team. We (Whitney our then head-of-cs and I) started it with the explicit goal of finding friction throughout our product and removing it. The team went and found things that caused customers to have problems and fixed them. We also focused on building tools that fully empowered our support team to understand customer requests and the context around their account.

At the center of our ethos was some really good feedback I got on a PR where I was extremely lazy and didn't put in the effort to make an internal tools page nice: `admins are people too`.

![A 2019 GitHub PR review thread. The reviewer asks "why vertical? it looks pretty silly in your screenshot" about a radio toggle on an admin card-limits form. I reply that I can write some CSS to make it look nicer, but didn't think it was really needed for the admin page. The reviewer responds: "admins are people too".](/assets/adminsPost/admins-are-people-too.png)

That reverberated into our mandate for several years, and is still great feedback we share internally.

A lot of our teams over the years have grown/shifted/moved/renamed, but we still have a dedicated product team who thinks about the CX layer every day. That team is staffed with some of our (in my, biased opinion since I helped start the OG team) best talent.
